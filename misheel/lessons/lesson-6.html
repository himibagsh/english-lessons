The three rules, again

1

Hearing a word is not hearing an answer.

2

Wait for the no. but · though · actually · used to · barely · I knew exactly · we hadn't gone there to.

3

On the first playing, answer only what you are sure of. Leave the rest blank and let the second playing decide — which it did for you on question 6 of the picture paper, the one time you tried it.

A different job, and it is also your ear

14 minutes

That was listening. This is writing, and it is not connected — except in one way, which is that you can hear it.

In the email you wrote on 9 October, six of your ten mistakes were the same mistake. Not articles. Not spelling. This one.
1our English teacher want us to learn
2I think the film do one thing very well
3The characters was very colourful
4so the story were happy to watch
5I think you dont likes this film
6small children who is under seven

Look at 3 and 4 together. In one you used a plural verb for one thing; in the next you used a singular verb for many things. You are not getting it wrong in one direction. You are guessing.

The whole rule

1

One thing: the verb takes an s. The film does. My teacher wants. The story is.

2

More than one thing: no s. The films do. My teachers want. The characters are.

3

It feels backwards, and that is why it is worth saying aloud. The s moves: the film works / the films work. One of them has the s on the noun, the other on the verb. Never both.

The one that catches everybody

When words come between the subject and the verb, your ear follows the nearest noun instead of the real one. The characters in the film … were, not was. Cover everything between and the answer appears.

Murphy 79.3. Ten items, every one of them a verb matching a subject — don't / doesn't, was / were, is / are, do / does. Tell me your score out of ten before we go on.

Now the uncomfortable question

If you scored well on those ten, then you know this rule. And you still made the same mistake six times in an email three weeks ago.

That is not a knowledge problem. It is what happens when the rule has to work while you are also choosing words, watching a clock and thinking about what to say next.

You have seen this before and it ended well. You scored full marks on two weeks of article exercises, dropped four articles in an email, and then articles stopped being a problem at all — fifty-eight right in a row. The exercises were never the thing that fixed it. Producing sentences under time was.

So today's test is not on paper

The next two stages are spoken and timed. Getting 79.3 right earns you nothing here.

Say each one right, out loud

15 minutes

Not written. Said. For each one: say the whole sentence correctly, then say why in four words — one film, so does.

Then the test that matters

Getting these right now proves nothing. You got every article exercise right for two weeks and still dropped four articles in an email. The next stage is the real one.

Ten sentences, fast

32 minutes

I give you a subject. You say a whole sentence about it, out loud, in under five seconds — then write it down. Speed is the point: this only counts if you have no time to think.

Then fifteen minutes on paper

Write one paragraph, about 80 words, answering this: what do most films get wrong about school?

Every sentence must have a subject that is either one thing or more than one thing — no I, no you, because those hide the problem. Underline every verb when you finish.
