Four jobs, four days

A student writes up a week of work experience

A

Our school makes everyone in Year 11 spend a week on work experience, and until this year I had thought of it as a week off. You find a company, you photocopy things for five days, and you come back with a form signed. I decided to do something different: instead of one placement I arranged four, one a day, and spent the fifth writing this.

B

Monday was a vet’s surgery, which I had wanted to see since I was nine. I lasted until eleven o’clock, when a dog arrived that had been hit by a car. Nobody asked me to help and I was glad. What surprised me was not the blood but the paperwork: the vet spent more of the morning typing than treating. She said that was the part nobody warns you about, and that she still would not do anything else.

C

Tuesday was a bakery, starting at half past four in the morning. I have never been so tired or so warm. By eight o’clock the day’s work was essentially finished, which I had not expected, and the baker was going home to sleep while my friends were arriving at school. Wednesday, at a software company, was the opposite: nobody came in before ten, nobody appeared to be doing anything in particular, and yet by the evening something had clearly been built.

D

Thursday I spent with my aunt, who teaches Year 3. I had assumed this would be the easy one because I know her, and it was the hardest by a distance. Thirty children need something from you every minute of the day, and what I could not get over was that she remembered all of it — who had fallen out with whom, who had not eaten. On Friday, writing this up, I realised the week had not told me what I want to do. It had told me something more useful: that every job looks completely different from inside.
